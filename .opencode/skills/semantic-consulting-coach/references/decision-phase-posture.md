# Decision-Phase posture: how a consultant behaves inside the boundary

Condensed from Meaningfy's Decision-Phase advisory practice (internal posture register), written for
senior consultants and leadership — **not a sales script and not a junior playbook**. Where
[`engagement-model.md`](engagement-model.md) states *what P1 is for*, this file states *how a
consultant behaves inside it*, especially under client pressure to give more than the boundary
allows.

The purpose: under pressure, Meaningfy consistently acts as a safe pair of hands — highly skilled,
calm, respectful, and firm in its professional boundaries.

## Load-bearing principles

- **Ambiguity is a signal, not a failure.** When a client is vague, contradictory, or unsure, that is
  not a weakness to compensate for — it is a signal that a decision has not yet been made. The task
  is not to resolve ambiguity immediately, but to hold it safely until it can be resolved
  deliberately.
- **Helpfulness vs. usefulness.** Helpfulness answers questions, explains concepts, reacts quickly.
  Usefulness enables the client to make a decision that holds over time. Inside the Decision Phase,
  usefulness always outranks helpfulness.
- **We do not guess.** No architectural sketches, sequencing recommendations, pilot definitions, or
  governance designs based on partial or informal understanding. When information is insufficient,
  say so — calmly and explicitly.
- **Calm under pressure.** Client urgency does not transfer to the consultant. The response to
  urgency is to slow the interaction down, not accelerate it. Calm is not passivity; calm is control.
- **No need to prove expertise.** If the urge to impress appears, the professional posture has
  already been left.
- **Protective, not defensive, formulations.** Say what is true about the situation, not what
  excuses declining: *"We can, but that would be guesswork at this stage."* · *"We could sketch
  something, but it would not be responsible without context."* · *"Giving you something quick now
  may cost you more later."*
- **Redirect, don't block.** Point to the path that creates real value instead of refusing flatly:
  *"The right way to create value here is to clarify a few decisions first."* · *"This is exactly
  what our Decision Phase is designed to address."*
- **Yes / not yet.** Say yes to orientation, explanation, and clarification. Say not yet to
  recommendations, sequencing, and commitments. "Not yet" is sequencing, not refusal.
- **End cleanly.** Summarise what is known, what is undecided, and what step would be required — then
  pause. Silence is an acceptable outcome; it is not a failure to fill.
- **Final principle.** Meaningfy is not here to move fast. It is here to move right.

## Internal red flags (pause and reassess)

- Answering the same contextual question repeatedly.
- Sketching solutions with no ownership on the client side.
- Feeling pressure to justify Meaningfy's value.
- The client avoiding decision authority or budget.

## Where the free/paid boundary shows up in this material

If a client asks for recommendations, sequencing, or scoping before committing to a paid stage, that
is the signal to pause and propose Discovery & Onboarding at the appropriate depth (see
[`docs/ai-sales/engagement-lifecycle.md`](../../../docs/ai-sales/engagement-lifecycle.md)
for which tier). This is the boundary between presale and paid value stated in coaching terms — the
commercial rule itself lives in `docs/ai-sales/engagement-lifecycle.md`, not here.
