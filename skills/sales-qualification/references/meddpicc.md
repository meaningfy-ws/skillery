# MEDDPICC and the prospect question bank

The one bank of questions Meaningfy asks prospects, and the qualification spine for every deal with an end client. (The coaching questions the coach asks the *user* are `semantic-consulting-coach`'s.) Each element is either **known** (with its source) or **OPEN** (with the question that will close it). Gaps become the questions of the next meeting plan.

| Element | What we need to know | Questions that close it |
|---|---|---|
| **M**etrics | how they will measure the benefit: hours saved, risk avoided, audit cost, time to answer | "How will you know this worked?" · "What does the current situation cost you each month?" · "What would success need to look like to be taken seriously?" |
| **E**conomic buyer | who signs and owns the budget | "Who else needs to agree before this goes ahead?" · "Whose budget would this come from?" |
| **D**ecision criteria | what they will judge options on | "What will make one option better than another for you?" · "What would make a first initiative clearly fail internally?" |
| **D**ecision process | the steps and dates to a decision | "What happens between today and a signed order?" · "When do you need it in place, and why then?" · "Is this exploratory, or is there pressure to act?" |
| **P**aper process | procurement, legal, security review, framework or tender rules | "Who in procurement or legal is involved, and how long does it usually take?" |
| **I**dentify pain | the pain, its cost, its urgency, in their words | "What happens if nothing changes in the next six months?" |
| *Initiative* (with Metrics) | the funded programme this blocks (FAIR, data platform, AI, regulation), its budget and its date | "What triggered this conversation now?" · "What is this blocking that leadership cares about?" · "Is there a budget line for it this year?" |
| **C**hampion | the person who wants this and sells it inside; their personal win and personal risk | "Who would gain most if this works?", then test: do they share information and open doors? · "What would have to be true for this to be an easy yes for you internally?" |
| **C**ompetition | other vendors, the in-house option, doing nothing, and **past attempts** | "What else are you considering, including doing it yourselves?" · "What have you already tried?" |

## Which questions when

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| A first encounter (conference, networking) | one or two of *First encounters* below, nothing else |
| A scheduled discovery call (30–60 min) | *Landscape and semantic pressure*, then the MEDDPICC questions that close the biggest gaps |
| Any later conversation | the MEDDPICC questions for the elements still OPEN |

### First encounters

- How do topics like data semantics, knowledge graphs or shared meaning show up in your organisation today?
- How important is data meaning for analytics or AI in your environment?
- Is meaning and context more of a governance concern, an architecture concern, or not really on the radar yet?
- When different teams talk about the same data, do definitions tend to align or diverge?

No conceptual signal or curiosity: Pain and Fit stay at 0 and the deal is not pursued now.

### Landscape and semantic pressure

- How would you describe your current data landscape: centralised, federated, mixed?
- Which initiatives struggle most with data reuse or interpretation?
- Do you have a data catalogue or metadata tooling today? How is it actually used?
- Where does data governance sit: centrally, per domain, or informally?
- Where do you see the biggest friction caused by inconsistent meaning?
- Which data crosses domains most often?
- What breaks first when definitions are unclear: reporting, integration, AI?

**Past attempts: the three pillars.** Map each attempt they describe onto the three pillars, and name the one it missed, without blaming them:
1. **a shared model**: a governed ontology or model, or only a glossary?
2. **repeatable mappings**: declarative and tested, or one-off scripts?
3. **governance**: owners and a change process, or did it die after the project?

**The verdict.** The five Ps ([five-ps](five-ps.md)) are read from these elements: Pain from *Pain*, Priority from *Initiative* and *Metrics*, Power from *Economic buyer* and *Champion*, Plan from *Decision process*, Fit from the offer and the disqualifiers.

Plus **users**: who will use the result day to day (they make or break adoption).

**For partner deals** (Consulting via Partner, Partnerships): qualify the partner too: who on their side chooses us, what they need from us (CVs, references, capability, price, availability), how they are paid, who owns the client conversation.

**For calls and tenders**: map MEDDPICC onto the call documents instead of "discovering" it: the paper process is the call itself (eligibility, lots, criteria, deadline); the economic buyer is the contracting authority or funder; competition is the other bidders.

**Not a questionnaire**: never fire the question list at an early prospect or a partner in a first conversation; pick the one or two questions that close the biggest gap.
