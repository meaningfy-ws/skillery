# Value equation, risk and blame, objection drivers

## Value equation (Hormozi)

Why the offer is worth taking for **this** client. Value rises with the dream outcome and with how likely they think they are to get it. It falls with the time it takes and the effort it costs.

| Lever | Question for this client | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome ↑ | What result do they want, in their words? | Meeting notes, mails, public statements |
| Perceived likelihood ↑ | Why would they believe we can deliver it? | The closest reference, a demo on their data, a partner's endorsement |
| Time delay ↓ | How fast is the first visible win? | First result in days or weeks; the discovery workshop gives an early read |
| Effort and sacrifice ↓ | How little must they do? | Done-for-you parts; their systems stay as they are |

Write it as one line in the dossier: *"For [client], [step named in their words] gets [outcome in their words] within [time], with [little effort], and we can show [proof]."*

Add **why us vs. the alternative** in one sentence: different, not just better than doing nothing, doing it in-house, or the usual vendor.

## Risk and blame

This section follows Rory Sutherland. Buyers in compliance, banking and the public sector rarely buy upside. They buy cover against being blamed. For every full dossier, write two things.

**What could go wrong for the champion.** For example:
- a failed project with their name on it;
- a small vendor that disappears;
- a lock-in they will have to defend;
- a budget overrun.

**The cover we give.** Lead with this, before features:
- a recognised reference, the closest public one we have;
- a partner's name around us;
- a fixed frame with stop points (`proposal-writing`);
- a small, reversible first step;
- open standards and no lock-in;
- handover documentation;
- named backup staff.

## Guarantees and commercial terms

Not this skill's to set. The safeguard options a client may be offered are owned by
`semantic-consulting-coach` (`engagement-lifecycle`); the fixed frame and stop points by
`proposal-writing`; price by `estimation`, inside the approved ranges `sales-operations` holds.
Any guarantee, bonus or price in a draft is *ask first*. Never guarantee an outcome that depends on
the client's data, staff or decisions, and never offer a guarantee in a B2G tender.

## Objection drivers

For the dossier, sort the client's likely objections by the value-equation driver they hit:
- *not worth it* (outcome);
- *won't work for us* (likelihood);
- *too slow* (time);
- *too hard* (effort);
- *risky for me* (blame).

Write each with its answer and the proof we have. The objection types, the library and how to answer in a conversation are owned by `sales-conversations`; the driver only says which lever of the offer to strengthen.

Typical objections in our market:
- "is it a toy, an AI wrapper?";
- "our data is messy";
- "we have no capacity";
- "projects take months";
- "will our data leak?";
- "a small vendor might disappear".

**Never** inflate value or promise results we cannot measure.
