# Conversation tools

The tools a meeting plan picks from. The principles, and when each source does not apply, are in [selling-method](selling-method.md). The after-meeting note says which tools were used.

## Discovery flow (adapted from Hormozi's C-L-O-S-E-R)

1. **Clarify** why they are here, in their words.
2. **Label** their problem.
3. **Overview** past attempts and what each missed, on the three pillars of the dossier's MEDDPICC (`sales-qualification`): a shared model, repeatable mappings, governance.
4. **Picture the outcome**: what changes for them, not our method.
5. **Address concerns**, with proof and cover.
6. **Agree the next step**, who must be involved, and a date.

The flow does not close in the room unless the decision can be taken there. Do not use it when the partner leads the meeting.

## Commercial teaching (Challenger)

For a first meeting with a new prospect, before discovery goes deep, when the dossier shows no clear pain yet. Six steps, from one insight in [insights](insights.md):
1. **Warmer**: show you understand their world: two or three challenges people in their role face, from the dossier and their profile. Aim for "yes, that's us".
2. **Reframe**: the insight. "What we see is that…"
3. **Rational drowning**: what it costs, with their own numbers or sourced ones; otherwise ask them for theirs.
4. **Emotional impact**: a story of an organisation like theirs living with it (anonymised unless we have consent).
5. **A new way**: what would have to be true to fix it, without naming us yet.
6. **Our solution**: how Meaningfy does that, then the small first step on the ladder.

Then switch to listening (Voss) for their reaction. Do not use it in tenders or when the partner leads the meeting.

## Rapport (Robbins)

Match the pace and style at the start, in live conversation only. People act for their reasons, not ours.

## Listening (Voss)

- **Mirror**: repeat the last one to three key words as a question.
- **Label**: "It sounds like…", "It seems that…".
- **Accusation audit**: name their likely fear before they do. Use it only when the dossier shows evidence that the fear exists, such as a voiced objection or a loss note. Otherwise give the reassurance (references, insurance, backup) without naming the fear.
- **No-oriented questions**: "Would it be a bad idea to…?" A "no" feels safe to say.
- **Aim for "that's right"**: summarise their situation so well they say it. Only then talk about the solution.
- **Calibrated questions** ("How…?", "What…?"):
  - "How does this fit what you're trying to achieve?"
  - "What's the biggest challenge here?"
  - "How am I supposed to do that?" (a graceful no)
- **Questions that change focus**: "What would have to be true for this to be an easy yes for you internally?"
- **People behind the table**: who can kill the deal, and who decides.

## Belief-breaking (Hormozi)

Their belief → why it doesn't hold → what is true instead → why it is true → the proof.
