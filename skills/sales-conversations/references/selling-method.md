# Selling method

The principles the agent sells by, and where each source applies. This file is the only statement of the method. [conversation-tools](conversation-tools.md), [email-patterns](email-patterns.md), [objections](objections.md) and the dossier's value equation apply it and point here.

Sources:
- Hormozi (*$100M Offers*, *Gym Launch Secrets*);
- Voss (*Never Split the Difference*);
- Robbins;
- Bloch;
- Rory Sutherland (*Alchemy*);
- Nassim Nicholas Taleb (*The Black Swan*, *Fooled by Randomness*);
- Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson (*The Challenger Sale*);
- MEDDPICC, and the five Ps derived from it (`sales-qualification`).

## Principles

1. **80% homework, 20% selling.** Know their situation before you speak. Every message proves the homework in one specific, sourced line.
2. **Genuine interest beats eagerness.** Ask more than you tell. Eagerness is a negative signal: a desperate pipeline must never sound desperate.
3. **Sell cover, not upside** (Sutherland). Lead with what makes the choice safe for the champion: reference, partner, fixed price, small reversible step, no lock-in. Do this before features.
4. **Signal with costly proof, not claims** (Sutherland). Show a sourced observation, a tailored one-page read, or named work such as the EU Publications Office or model2owl. Never write "leading", "innovative" or "cutting-edge".
5. **Shrink the yes** (Sutherland, Hormozi). The first step is small, fixed-price and reversible, and is credited to the next step.
6. **Own the frame and the name** (Sutherland). Describe the offer in the client's problem language and moment ("merger definitions alignment, 2 days"), not in our jargon (knowledge graph, ontology). Test: would the champion use this phrase with their boss?
7. **Arm the satisficer** (Sutherland). Give one recommended option, with at most one fallback, and a paragraph (answer first, per `executive-communication`) the champion can forward internally unchanged.
8. **Try small, human, counter-intuitive moves first** (Sutherland). Before rewriting a sequence, try a phone call instead of a mail, owning a delay, a different sender, or a sharper subject.
9. **Value, not price** (Hormozi). Apply the value equation and the risk-and-blame cover from the dossier. Never discount; trade scope, timing, or a bonus that removes a named objection.
10. **Teach, tailor, take control** (Dixon and Adamson, *The Challenger Sale*). Lead a first touch or a first meeting with one commercial insight from [insights](insights.md): a reframe of their own problem they had not seen, tailored to their role and to what their profile says they care about. Then take control of the next step and of the money conversation, calmly and with reasons: constructive tension, never pressure. Teaching earns the right to ask; it is not a pitch.
11. **Tactical empathy** (Voss). Listen first: label, mirror, ask calibrated questions, aim for "that's right". Only then talk about the solution.
12. **Rapport and the other person's reasons** (Robbins). People act for their reasons, not ours. Read what moves each person, from what they said and did: reward, a cause they believe in, recognition, safety. Record it in their profile (`sales-accounts`, *How they decide*) and speak to it honestly. The owner's state and preparation are their own work, not client-facing text.
13. **Notes and speed** (Bloch). Notes during the meeting; the follow-up the same day; actions into Odoo.

## Decisions under uncertainty (Taleb)

Taleb's lens works in two directions: on how clients decide, and on our own judgement.

**How clients decide.**
- Buyers fear the rare blow-up more than they value the upside. Offer small, capped, reversible first steps whose worst case is known (the discovery workshop); never ask for a large irreversible bet.
- Skin in the game: show what we risk with them (fixed price, the next step credited, a handover so they are never locked in).
- People explain their past choices with tidy stories. Ask what actually happened before you accept the story (who decided, what nearly went wrong).

**Our own judgement.**
- **Narrative fallacy and confirmation bias.** A neat story about why a deal will close is a warning. Every dossier names the evidence against winning, and the weekly review looks for it.
- **Silent evidence.** Learn from lost and stalled deals, not only from wins; the weekly review reads them.
- **No false precision.** Probabilities and expected revenue are ranges with their basis, never a single confident number without one.
- **Black swans.** A deal's fate can turn on one event outside it (a reorganisation, a budget freeze, a champion leaving). Two contacts in each account, and a relationship that does not depend on one person, are the cover.

## Personal touches

People are people. A detail someone shared with us, remembered and used well, shows attention; used badly it feels like surveillance.
- Use one only when it is natural, at most one per message, and never in the first message to someone.
- Only what the person shared with us or published themselves (`sales-accounts`, personal details). Never let a message reveal something they did not tell us.
- Occasions they shared (a birthday, a launch, a promotion) are a short, warm note from the owner on the day, with no ask in it.
- In a tender, or with a buyer bound by procurement rules, no gifts and no personal favours; a kind word is fine.

## Where each source is not used

- **Hormozi**: not in B2G tenders, and not in a hype register with compliance, CFO or bank buyers. In partner-owned deals, the partner sets the offer and the frame.
- **Voss**: never a buyer-side price ladder (offering 65 → 85 → 95 → 100% of a target is a buyer's method). No tactics in written tender clarifications. Accusation audit only when there is evidence that the fear exists.
- **Robbins**: mirroring only in live conversation, never mimicry in writing.
- **Sutherland**: never a licence to skip evidence where a scoring grid decides. Reframing must never mislead.
- **MEDDPICC**: not a question list fired at early prospects or partners. In tenders, map it onto the call documents.
- **Five Ps**: a verdict for us, never shown to the client; not used in tenders, partnerships or framework work orders, which have their own scores.
- **Challenger**: not in B2G tenders (the scoring grid leads) and not over a partner's frame (offer the insight to the partner, who decides whether to use it with their client). Never teach with numbers we cannot source.
- **Power and intelligence frameworks** (Robert Greene's *48 Laws of Power*, the MICE model of money, ideology, coercion and ego): not used. Their benign part (reward, cause, recognition) is already covered above; coercion, leverage of compromising information, deception, flattery used to manipulate, and anything romantic are never used, whoever asks.
- **The discovery flow** (formerly C-L-O-S-E-R): not in three-party partner meetings, and not in first executive meetings.

## Price

- **Who owns price.** Effort and price come from `estimation` and `proposal-writing`; this skill only uses them.
- **Direct clients.** Anchor on the cost of doing nothing, then give one recommended option at a precise price, once the goal is known and only with a price the owner approved for the deal.
- **Partners and frameworks.** When they ask for sizes and prices, give the approved ranges (`price_ranges` in `sales-config.yaml`, see `sales-operations`); with none set, every price is *ask first*. Withholding price from a reseller blocks the deal.
- **Concessions** are non-monetary: scope, timing, a reference role. Never split the difference; never discount.

## Cadence

Owned here and applied in [email-patterns](email-patterns.md); the tone of each touch is the `outreach-email` channel of `meaningfy-voice`.
- At most 3 touches without a reply, then close the loop and stop.
- At most one message per contact per 7 days.
- Every touch adds something: an insight, a sourced observation, a useful pointer.
