# Selling method

The only statement of the method and of where each source applies; the other references and the dossier's value equation apply it.

## Principles

1. **80% homework, 20% selling.** Know their situation before you speak. Every message proves the homework in one specific, sourced line.
2. **Genuine interest beats eagerness.** A desperate pipeline must never sound desperate.
3. **Sell cover, not upside** (Sutherland). Lead with what makes the choice safe for the champion: reference, partner, fixed price, small reversible step, no lock-in. Do this before features.
4. **Signal with costly proof, not claims** (Sutherland). Show a sourced observation, a tailored one-page read, or named work such as the EU Publications Office or model2owl. Never write "leading", "innovative" or "cutting-edge".
5. **Shrink the yes** (Sutherland, Hormozi). The first step is small, fixed-price and reversible, and is credited to the next step.
6. **Own the frame and the name** (Sutherland). Describe the offer in the client's problem language and moment ("merger definitions alignment, 2 days"), not in our jargon (knowledge graph, ontology). Test: would the champion use this phrase with their boss?
7. **Arm the satisficer** (Sutherland). One recommended option, at most one fallback, and a paragraph (answer first, per `executive-communication`) the champion can forward internally unchanged.
8. **Try small, human, counter-intuitive moves first** (Sutherland). Before rewriting a sequence, try a phone call instead of a mail, owning a delay, a different sender, or a sharper subject.
9. **Value, not price** (Hormozi). Apply the value equation and the risk-and-blame cover from the dossier. Never discount; trade scope, timing, or a bonus that removes a named objection.
10. **Teach, tailor, take control** (Challenger). Lead a first touch or a first meeting with one insight from [insights](insights.md), tailored to their role and to what their profile says they care about. Then take control of the next step and of the money conversation, calmly and with reasons: constructive tension, never pressure.
11. **Tactical empathy** (Voss). Listen first ([conversation-tools](conversation-tools.md)); only then talk about the solution.
12. **The other person's reasons** (Robbins). Read what moves each person from what they said and did: reward, a cause they believe in, recognition, safety. Record it in their profile (`sales-accounts`, *How they decide*) and speak to it honestly. The owner's state and preparation are their own work, not client-facing text.
13. **Notes and speed** (Bloch). Notes during the meeting; actions into Odoo.

## Decisions under uncertainty (Taleb)

**How clients decide.** Buyers fear the rare blow-up more than they value the upside: never ask for a large irreversible bet; the first step's worst case is known (the discovery workshop), and we show what we risk with them (principles 3 and 5). Ask what actually happened (who decided, what nearly went wrong) before you accept their tidy story of a past choice.

**Our own judgement.**
- A neat story about why a deal will close is a warning: the weekly review looks for the dossier's evidence against winning (`sales-qualification`).
- Learn from lost and stalled deals, not only from wins; the weekly review reads them.
- Probabilities and expected revenue are ranges with their basis, never a single confident number.
- One event outside a deal (a reorganisation, a budget freeze, a champion leaving) can turn it. Two contacts in each account, and a relationship that does not depend on one person, are the cover.

## Personal touches

- Use one only when it is natural, at most one per message, and never in the first message to someone.
- Only what the person shared with us or published themselves (`sales-accounts`, personal details). Never let a message reveal something they did not tell us.
- Occasions they shared (a birthday, a launch, a promotion) are a short, warm note from the owner on the day, with no ask in it.
- In a tender, or with a buyer bound by procurement rules, no gifts and no personal favours; a kind word is fine.

## Channel matrix

| Channel | What leads | Allowed | Not used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B2G tender** (Calls & Tenders) | The call's rules and scoring grid | Evidence per criterion, references, clear compliance | Scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, Voss tactics in written clarifications, framing tricks, any contact outside the clarification channel |
| **Direct B2B** (Direct Consulting) | The client's pain and the champion's risk | All principles; value equation; the discovery flow; guarantees only from the safeguard options of `semantic-consulting-coach` (`engagement-lifecycle`), after the owner's yes | Hype register with compliance, CFO or bank buyers; closing in the room when no decision can be taken there |
| **Partner-led** (Consulting via Partner, FWC) | The partner's frame and the partner's client relationship | Partner reviews client text first; approved price ranges; making the partner's account manager look good | Contacting the client behind the partner; quoting below the partner; our own offer frame over theirs |
| **Partnerships** | Mutual value and joint targets | Capability statements, references, joint target lists | Pressure; treating the partner like a buyer |

## Where each source is not used

The channel matrix already rules out tender tactics and our frame over a partner's; in addition:

- **Hormozi**: not in a hype register with compliance, CFO or bank buyers.
- **Voss**: never a buyer-side price ladder (65 → 85 → 95 → 100% of a target is a buyer's method). The accusation audit's limit is in [conversation-tools](conversation-tools.md).
- **Robbins**: matching pace and style only in live conversation, never mimicry in writing.
- **Sutherland**: never a licence to skip evidence where a scoring grid decides. Reframing must never mislead.
- **MEDDPICC**: not a question list fired at early prospects or partners. In tenders, map it onto the call documents.
- **Five Ps**: a verdict for us, never shown to the client; not used in tenders, partnerships or framework work orders, which have their own scores.
- **Challenger**: over a partner's frame, offer the insight to the partner, who decides whether to use it with their client. Numbers follow the rules in [insights](insights.md).
- **Power and intelligence frameworks** (Greene's *48 Laws of Power*, the MICE model): not used. Their benign part (reward, cause, recognition) is covered above; coercion, leverage of compromising information, deception, manipulative flattery, and anything romantic are never used, whoever asks.
- **The discovery flow** (formerly C-L-O-S-E-R): not in three-party partner meetings, and not in first executive meetings.

## Price

- Effort and price come from `estimation` and `proposal-writing`; this skill only uses them.
- **Direct clients.** Anchor on the cost of doing nothing, then give one recommended option at a precise price, once the goal is known and only with a price the owner approved for the deal.
- **Partners and frameworks.** When they ask for sizes and prices, give the approved ranges (the offer catalogue named by `offer_catalogue` in `sales-config.yaml`, see `sales-operations`); a price it does not list is *ask first*. Withholding price from a reseller blocks the deal.
- **Concessions** are non-monetary: scope, timing, a reference role. Never split the difference; never discount.

## Cadence

The tone of each touch is the `outreach-email` channel of `meaningfy-voice`.
- At most 3 touches without a reply, then close the loop and stop.
- At most one message per contact per 7 days.
- Every touch adds something: an insight, a sourced observation, a useful pointer.
