# Commercial insights

The insights Meaningfy teaches (Challenger: *teach, tailor, take control*, see [selling-method](selling-method.md)). A commercial insight is a reframe: it shows prospects something about their own problem that they had not seen, and it leads to what we do best without naming us first.

Every insight here is a position Meaningfy takes. In a message it is stated as our view, with proof where we have it, never as a statistic we cannot source. Tailor it to the person's role (the table's last column) and to what their profile and dossier say they care about. One insight per message, the one that fits them best.

## Commercial insights

| # | Insight (the reframe) | What they usually believe | Proof we can show | Opening question | Tailored for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **FAIR stalls at the I.** Findable and accessible is the platform's job; interoperable and reusable is where programmes stall, because nobody settled what the data means. | "We bought a FAIR / data platform, so we are on track." | the eProcurement Ontology for the EU Publications Office (many stakeholders, one model); the Mapping Workbench | "Which term means something different in two of your systems?" | head of FAIR or data strategy; R&D data leads |
| 2 | **AI does not scale on data that disagrees.** A pilot works on one dataset; production fails when the same entity means different things in different systems. | "We need a better model or more data." | semantic grounding: answers traced to governed meaning | "What happened to the last AI pilot that touched data from two systems?" | AI sponsors; heads of data science |
| 3 | **Meaning is renegotiated in every integration script.** Each new interface re-decides what a compound, a customer or a contract means, so integration cost grows with every system. | "Integration is an engineering backlog." | model-first engineering: one model generates schemas, OWL, SHACL and documentation | "Where do your mappings live today: in people's heads, in scripts, in a tool?" | heads of data architecture; IT leads |
| 4 | **The platform does F and A; the meaning is yours.** No vendor can decide what your core entities mean for you; that decision is a capability you own, or a dependency you rent. | "Our vendor will handle semantics." | vendor-independent delivery; the client keeps the model and the tooling | "How does your platform handle two systems that define the same entity differently?" | platform owners; CDOs |
| 5 | **Mappings are assets, not scripts.** Declarative, tested mappings are reused; one-off scripts are rewritten at every change. | "Mapping is a one-time migration task." | the Mapping Workbench (open source); RML mappings at scale | "What does it cost you each time a source system changes its schema?" | data engineering leads |
| 6 | **Regulation asks for traceability you cannot add afterwards.** DORA, the AI Act and audit questions need to know where an answer came from; that has to be designed into the data, not bolted onto the report. | "Compliance is a reporting task." | the semantic layer as the record of meaning and provenance | "If an auditor asked where this figure came from, how long would the answer take?" | compliance, risk and finance leads |

## Rules

- **Source the numbers.** If the reframe uses a cost or a figure, it is the client's own number, a published one with its source, or left out. No invented statistics.
- **Teach, then ask.** The insight opens a question; it never ends in a pitch of our services in the same message.
- **Add insights carefully.** A new insight, or a change to one, is a decision card in the weekly review whose yes is filing it as a skillery issue (this file changes only through skillery), with where it came from (a won deal, a lost deal, a recurring objection).
