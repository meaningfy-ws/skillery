Absorbs ambiguity

Makes the next step explicit and defensible

Governance support

Semantic operations

Capability building

Continuous evolution

This protects:

client success,

intellectual capital,

and long-term trust.

How to use this sheet
This sheet supports pre-sales conversations, not selling.

At conferences / networking events → use Section A only

On scheduled discovery calls (30–60 min) → use Sections B and C

Do not force all sections; use what fits the context

The goal is to decide whether to: stop, schedule a focused follow-up, or propose the Decision Phase.

A. Conceptual calibration (first encounters only)
Use 1–2 questions.

How do topics like data semantics, knowledge graphs, or shared meaning show up in your organisation today?

How important is data meaning for analytics or AI in your environment?

Is meaning and context more of a governance concern, an architecture concern, or not really on the radar yet?

When different teams talk about the same data, do definitions tend to align or diverge?

Rule: If there is no conceptual signal or curiosity → de-prioritise.

B. Technical & organisational discovery (discovery calls)
How would you describe your current data landscape – centralised, federated, mixed?

Which initiatives struggle most with data reuse or interpretation?

Do you have a data catalogue or metadata tooling today? How is it actually used?

Where does data governance sit – centrally, per domain, or informally?

Semantic & interoperability pressure
Where do you see the biggest friction caused by inconsistent meaning?

Which data crosses domains most often?

What breaks first when definitions are unclear – reporting, integration, AI?

C. Business readiness & decision dynamics (discovery calls)
What triggered this conversation now?

If nothing changes in the next 12 months, what is the risk?

Who would ultimately need to support or approve further work?

Is this currently exploratory, or is there pressure to act?

What has already been tried in this space?

What would make a first initiative clearly fail internally?

What would success need to look like to be taken seriously?

Internal decision (after the conversation)
Choose one:

De-prioritise – no pressure, no authority, no intent

Deepen discovery – relevance but unclear readiness

Introduce the Decision Phase – clear pain, urgency, or sequencing questions

Reminder
If the conversation moves from:

“Is this relevant?” → “What should we do?”

pre-sales has reached its boundary.

Propose a disciplined next step, not more discussion.

Governing Thought
You are not selling “semantics”, “advisory”, or “pilots”.
You are selling the moment when uncertainty becomes safe to commit.

Everything else — ontology, governance, architecture, pilots — only exists after that moment.

Once you internalise this, the pressure to explain, persuade, and demonstrate drops sharply, because your value is no longer “look how clever we are”, but:

“We help organisations decide what is worth building, without locking themselves into the wrong thing.”

“When do I stop explaining and say: now pay?”
Free: helping the client recognise that a problem exists.

Paid: helping the client decide what to do about it.

 

The three cognitive states of your clients
State 1 – Orientation (free)
“Is this even relevant to us?”

Vocabulary clarification

High-level explanations

Analogies

Examples from other industries

“What is a semantic layer?”

“Why Purview is not enough on its own”

✅ You can do this generously
❌ You must not customise it deeply

This is where teaching mode is allowed, but shallow.

State 2 – Decision pressure (paid)
“If we do this, what exactly do we do first — and what do we NOT do?”

This is where:

scoping begins,

trade-offs appear,

internal politics matter,

sequencing matters,

mistakes become expensive.

This is where Meaningfy’s real value starts.

If they ask any of the following, you are already in paid territory:

“Which domain should we start with?”

“Is marketing data the right entry point?”

“Should semantics live in governance or architecture?”

“What should we do before building a pilot?”

“How do we avoid another shelfware initiative?”

If you answer these for free, you are leaking intellectual capital.

State 3 – Execution (paid, different shape)
“Build this with us.”

Ontology, pipelines, pilots, tooling — all fine only after State 2 is completed.

“What is the unit of knowledge worth paying for?”
You sell Decision-Readiness, not advice.
Decision-readiness means:

the organisation knows what to do next,

why it is doing it,

what success looks like,

and what comes later.

This is not vague advisory.
It is a deliverable state.

Your missing product: the “Decision Phase”
(Service/offering)

Call it something like (working names):
Semantic Readiness & Direction Phase

Semantic & Governance Alignment Phase

Decision Foundation for a Semantic Layer

Semantic Architecture & Scope Definition

Name matters less than structure.

What this phase explicitly includes (and ends with)
This phase is paid, time-boxed, and outcome-bound.

Inputs (what they give you)
Access to key people (analytics, governance, IT)

Existing documentation (even messy)

One or two candidate domains

Activities (what you do)
Structured discovery workshops (not open-ended)

Data & governance landscape reading (not full audit)

Semantic scoping (what needs shared meaning vs what doesn’t)

Option framing (2–3 credible paths, not 10)

Risk & dependency surfacing

Outputs (this is the key)
One Decision Package, containing:

A clear recommendation for a first semantic initiative

Defined scope boundaries (what is explicitly out)

A sequenced roadmap (pilot → scale)

Explicit buy / build / defer decisions

A ready-to-contract execution brief

This is the moment where you can say, calmly:

“From here, we can execute — or you can take this and execute with someone else.”

That sentence is power.

Why this solves your psychological pressure
Let’s address your deepest frustration directly.

Today, you feel:
pressured to demonstrate,

forced to explain endlessly,

unsure when you’ve “done enough”.

With this structure:
You stop explaining when questions turn contextual.

You stop persuading once they ask for recommendations.

You stop chasing because the next step is concrete and paid.

Instead of:

“Let me explain more so you believe us”

You say:

“That question belongs in the decision phase. We do that as a short, paid engagement.”

No defensiveness. No apology.

“Decission phase” business structure
Use Fixed price + fixed duration Decision Phase

With explicit variable depth

And formal stop / extend mechanics
